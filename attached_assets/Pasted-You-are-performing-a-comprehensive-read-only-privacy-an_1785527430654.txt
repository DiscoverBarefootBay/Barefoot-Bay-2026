You are performing a comprehensive, read-only privacy and website-tracking audit of the BarefootBay.com application and its production configuration.

IMPORTANT CONSTRAINTS

1. Do not modify, delete, deploy, reconfigure, or refactor anything.
2. Do not install new analytics, consent, security, or monitoring packages.
3. Do not commit changes other than creating the requested Markdown audit report.
4. Do not expose API keys, authentication tokens, database credentials, private user information, complete cookie values, or other secrets in the report.
5. Redact all identifiers and sample payload values.
6. Use test accounts and synthetic test data only.
7. Clearly distinguish confirmed facts, reasonable inferences, unknowns, and items that require manual verification.
8. This is a technical and factual audit for review by qualified legal counsel. Do not make definitive legal conclusions.

BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE

BarefootBay.com uses a custom tracking system. The site owner’s law firm has asked for an assessment following the July 14, 2026 decision in D.B. et al. v. Bellesa Enterprises US, Inc.

The relevant concerns from that decision and the related client alert include:

- Whether website activity is collected or intercepted.
- Whether URLs, page titles, content names, searches, viewing history, form activity, or similar information are transmitted.
- Whether activity is associated with an account, email address, user ID, cookie ID, session ID, IP address, browser fingerprint, device information, or another identifier.
- Whether information is transmitted to Google, Meta, TikTok, advertising companies, analytics providers, hosting providers, logging providers, or other third parties.
- Whether nonessential tracking begins before a user affirmatively consents.
- Whether the privacy policy and consent interface explicitly identify the tracking tools, recipients, data categories, purposes, and data-sharing practices.
- Whether statements such as “we do not sell or share personal information” conflict with the site’s actual behavior.
- Whether rejecting or withdrawing consent actually stops tracking.
- Whether potentially sensitive browsing or content-access information is involved.

The fact that a tracking system is custom or first-party does not by itself resolve these questions. Determine what the system actually does.

PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Create a detailed factual report that allows the website owner and legal counsel to understand:

1. Every tracking, analytics, logging, advertising, personalization, session-recording, error-monitoring, and telemetry mechanism in the application.
2. Exactly what information each mechanism collects.
3. Where the information is sent.
4. Whether it is connected or connectable to an identifiable user, household, account, device, or browser.
5. Whether tracking is appropriately controlled by the consent mechanism.
6. Whether the privacy policy and other notices accurately describe the implementation.
7. What technical changes would reduce privacy and litigation risk.

B. CUSTOM TRACKING SYSTEM REVIEW

Locate and document the complete custom tracking implementation.

Determine:

- Where tracking events are created.
- What causes each event to fire.
- The complete event schema.
- Whether events include page URLs, route names, page titles, content titles, search terms, form names, button names, record IDs, listing IDs, message IDs, or user-generated content.
- Whether query-string parameters or URL fragments are captured.
- Whether form field values are captured.
- Whether passwords, authentication tokens, payment information, health information, precise location, private messages, or other sensitive fields could be captured accidentally.
- Whether account IDs, names, emails, phone numbers, addresses, membership information, or other direct identifiers are included.
- Whether cookie IDs, session IDs, IP addresses, user-agent strings, screen dimensions, language, operating system, browser version, referrer, or other device/browser attributes are included.
- Whether identifiers are persistent across sessions.
- Whether anonymous events are later connected to a registered user.
- Whether data is stored only in BarefootBay-controlled infrastructure.
- Whether data is forwarded, exported, synchronized, backed up, logged, or otherwise disclosed to an outside provider.
- Where the tracking database is hosted.
- Who has access.
- Retention periods and deletion procedures.
- Whether tracking data is included in backups.
- Whether tracking data can be exported or downloaded.
- Whether deleted user accounts remain represented in tracking records.
- Whether IP addresses or other identifiers are truncated, hashed, encrypted, or stored in plain text.
- Whether hashing is reversible or linkable using other available data.

Trace the entire data lifecycle:

browser → frontend code → API request → server processing → database/logging → reporting dashboard → exports/integrations → backups/deletion

D. RUNTIME AND NETWORK TESTING

Where technically possible, inspect the live site and local application using browser developer tools or equivalent network inspection.

Test at least these states:

1. New visitor with no existing cookies.
2. Before interacting with a consent banner.
3. After selecting “Reject,” “Decline,” or the most privacy-protective option.
4. After selecting analytics only, if offered.
5. After selecting advertising only, if offered.
6. After selecting “Accept All.”
7. Logged-out user.
8. Logged-in test user.
9. After consent is withdrawn or preferences are changed.
10. After cookies and local storage are cleared.

For each test, record:

- Requests made.
- Request recipient/domain.
- Request timing.
- Cookies created or read.
- Local-storage and session-storage entries.
- Beacon, fetch, XHR, image, script, WebSocket, and server-side calls.
- Redacted request payloads and query parameters.
- Whether the site functions after nonessential tracking is rejected.
- Whether rejected tracking reappears after navigation or refresh.
- Whether the consent choice persists correctly.
- Whether mobile and desktop behavior differ.

Never include live personal data or complete identifiers in the report.

E. CONSENT-MECHANISM REVIEW

Determine whether the site has a consent banner or consent-management platform.

Document:

- Which users and regions see it.
- Whether it appears before nonessential tracking begins.
- Whether “Accept” and “Reject” are equally clear and accessible.
- Whether choices are granular, such as necessary, analytics, personalization, and advertising.
- Whether optional categories are disabled by default.
- Whether the user takes an affirmative action before optional tracking begins.
- Whether consent is recorded with date, time, policy version, categories, and consent-interface version.
- Whether consent can be withdrawn as easily as it was given.
- Whether withdrawing consent stops future collection.
- Whether previously created optional cookies are removed.
- Whether server-side tracking honors the same preference.
- Whether logged-in and logged-out preferences are handled consistently.
- Whether Global Privacy Control signals are detected and respected.
- Whether any tool marked “strictly necessary” is actually used for analytics, advertising, profiling, or measurement.

H. SECURITY AND DATA-MINIMIZATION REVIEW

Evaluate whether the tracker collects more data than is needed for its documented purpose.

Consider:

- Removing complete URLs where route names would suffice.
- Removing query strings and fragments.
- Replacing content titles with nonsensitive internal categories.
- Avoiding form-field capture.
- Avoiding direct identifiers in analytics events.
- Separating account data from analytics data.
- Rotating or shortening anonymous identifiers.
- Truncating or avoiding IP storage.
- Reducing retention.
- Aggregating statistics.
- Limiting administrator access.
- Encrypting data in transit and at rest.
- Creating deletion procedures.
- Preventing sensitive information from entering application logs or error reports.
- Adding a technical kill switch for optional tracking.
